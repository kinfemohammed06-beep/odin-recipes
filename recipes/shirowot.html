Shiro Wot Recipe

Home

Description

Shiro Wat, alio known as Shuro or Shiro Wot, is a staple in any Ethiopian vegetarian platter. It’s a rich and creamy chickpea stew simmered slowly and served over traditional sour flatbread, injera, and a host of other vegetarian dishes. Each bite brings me back to the first time I tried it in Addis Ababa, making it a beloved favorite in my kitchen

ingredients

  • chickpea flour
  • berbere
  • ground cardamom
  • cumin powder
  • garlic powder
  • salt
  • olive oil
  • onion
  • garlic
  • tomato
  • water
  • injera

Steps

  1. If making your own blend from scratch, dry roast ¾ cup of chickpea flour in a large skillet along with 1 tablespoon of berbere spice, 1 teaspoon ground cardamom, 1 teaspoon cumin powder, and 1 teaspoon garlic powder. Set aside. (Note: You can skip this step if you are using store-bought shiro powder).
  2. In a medium-sized pot or Dutch oven, heat ⅓ cup of olive oil over low to medium heat. Add the diced onion and cook for 1–2 minutes just to get the aromatics fragrant—do not let the onions brown.
  3. Stir in 1 teaspoon of minced garlic and 1 tablespoon of tomato paste, sautéing for about 30 seconds until fragrant.
  4. Add the shiro powder, salt, and 3 cups of water to the pot, stirring well to prevent lumps. Bring the mixture to a boil over medium heat, then lower the heat to a low-medium simmer.
  5. Let it simmer for 20 to 30 minutes, stirring every 5 minutes to ensure it doesn't stick. The shiro wat is done when the oil floats to the top. Adjust salt to taste and serve hot with injera or rice.
